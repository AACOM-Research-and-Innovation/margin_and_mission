# Margin and Mission language and rule changes

November 2, 2026

We have revised the simulation language so participants see the same terms when choosing investments, reviewing results and comparing standings. The activity retains 15 investment cards, seven events and three simulated years. This record brings together the approved card descriptions, financial labels, briefing terminology, submission instructions and related rule corrections.

Three terminology categories remain open for discussion: decision-making roles, the name of the Board Ask exercise and the person making the board ruling. Yield language is also unchanged. These are separate from the changes implemented below.

| Previously used | Standard wording | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fund; Investment Fund; capital | Investment fund | One-time startup balance that carries forward without an annual refill. |
| Liquid reserve; reserve; reserves | Operating reserve | Cash that absorbs annual operating results and event cash effects. |
| Operating margin; margin; annual surplus or deficit | Operating result with operating surplus or operating deficit as appropriate | Annual operating revenue less annual operating expenses. The plan and the recorded year-end result are identified separately. |
| Tail; recurring obligation; recurring commitments | Annual expense or existing annual expenses | Costs that continue after purchase. Annual savings are labeled separately. |
| Startup; one-time cost | Startup cost paid once | Initial cost of an investment. The card price distinguishes it from annual expense. |

The financial summary separates the investment fund, operating result, starting restricted gift and operating reserve. A compact summary remains visible while browsing. Startup costs and annual expenses are explicitly labeled. For the rural investment, the orange flag says Starting restricted gift eligible; other cards do not repeat their ordinary funding source.

Red interaction flags retain short card-number references, including Pays out only alongside card 3 and Requires card 12 or delivers 60% revenue. Green flags identify delayed annual revenue. Results refer to relevant investments by recognizable short names and card numbers where appropriate. The borrowing-cap and yield narrative is preserved as an explicit exception.

### Meaning of the institutional research finding

Buying the data infrastructure and institutional research investment in Year 1 provides one extra institution-specific information item in the Year 2 and Year 3 briefings. Buying it in Year 2 provides the extra item in Year 3. Buying it in Year 3 provides no later briefing within the activity. The information supports interpretation of the situation; it is not a guarantee that an event will occur.

### Meaning of delayed benefits

The revenue rule remains purchase year plus the investment's configured lag. The AI dual pathway and biomedical bridge purchased in Year 1 begin revenue in Year 2. Advancement purchased in Year 1 begins its modeled annual revenue in Year 3. Branch-campus revenue begins beyond the exercise.

The generic points-and-protection sentence has been removed from non-revenue cards. The current model awards investment points at purchase and generally checks ownership when applying event protection. The lag fields on OMM succession and rural training do not delay those effects. No new maturation rule has been introduced.

## Operating reserve and the two gifts

The operating reserve begins at $13 million, separately from the $5 million investment fund. Annual operating surpluses add cash, annual deficits reduce cash, and events can add or remove cash. In this simplified model, operating results are treated as cash generated or used. At baseline expense levels, the opening reserve covers approximately 98 days of operations without revenue.

For example, without purchases or events, the starting $2.5 million annual surplus raises the reserve from $13 million to $15.5 million. Startup purchases ordinarily reduce the investment fund rather than the operating reserve. The rural-track startup is paid from the starting restricted gift while its annual expense still reduces the operating result.

| Feature | Starting restricted gift | Year 3 estate gift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline amount | $1.5 million available at the start | $4 million potential event gift |
| When available | Before the first investment decision | Only if the Year 3 event conditions produce full or partial capture |
| Current eligible use or effect | Rural-track startup cost of $500,000 | Full capture adds $2.5 million to operating reserve and reduces rural-track annual expense by $250,000 |
| Relationship to other balances | Separate tracked balance; it does not replenish annually | Does not refill the starting restricted gift or investment fund |
| Partial outcome | Any available eligible startup amount is used first | Holding either advancement or rural track produces $1.2 million in reserve cash; neither produces no gift |

Full estate-gift capture requires both the advancement officer and the rural track. The estate is described as restricted to rural health, but the engine places the spendable $2.5 million in the general operating reserve and does not separately enforce a purpose restriction on that cash. It also does not separately track the remaining $1.5 million of the headline gift. The previous claim that most of the corpus is restricted has therefore been replaced with an explicit description of the modeled cash and annual-expense effects. The gift amounts and accounting destinations have not changed. The $250,000 annual-expense reduction is applied after the Year 3 operating result is calculated. It changes annual expenses going forward; it does not retroactively increase the reported Year 3 operating result.

Two model questions remain for discussion. First, the starting gift is $1.5 million but the only eligible investment currently uses $500,000, leaving $1 million without another eligible use. Second, the estate narrative and its treatment as general reserve cash need a deliberate interpretation if more detailed donor restrictions are intended. These questions are documented rather than resolved through an unapproved accounting change.

## Rule corrections approved with the language changes

### Financial health includes the unspent investment fund

Unspent investment funds now contribute up to 5 points within the existing 35-point financial-health maximum. Reserve-balance points decrease from 15 to 10. This allocation was expressly approved; the other score-category maxima are unchanged.

| Financial-health component | Previous maximum | Current maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating reserve balance against $20 million | 15 | 10 |
| Unspent investment fund against the starting $5 million | 0 | 5 |
| Days cash on hand against 120 days | 10 | 10 |
| Cumulative operating result | 10 | 10 |
| Total before safeguards | 35 | 35 |

Before safeguards, each $1 million remaining in the investment fund contributes one financial-health point, up to five. A remaining balance of $2.5 million contributes 2.5 points. Existing liquidity and accreditation safeguards continue to apply and can reduce or eliminate financial-health points. The fund balance does not add points to mission, strategic position or resilience.

### No penalty for the no-exposure AI outcome

The AI-scrutiny outcome labeled No material effect no longer subtracts one mission point when the table has neither an AI-related investment requiring remediation nor governance. Its explanation now states that there is no financial or score change from that event. The other AI outcomes are unchanged.

### OMM outcome and total score wording

The OMM protection outcome now says Your earlier succession investment provided a qualified replacement. The unsupported statement that the investment was made three years ago has been removed. The final result displays Total score followed by the value, without of 100. Category maxima remain visible; facilitator adjustments still affect the total. The existing internal cap of 120 is unchanged.

The reference prepared portfolio now scores 56.6 and the do-nothing portfolio 17.0, compared with 57.7 and 13.9 before these rule corrections. These are regression scenarios, not expected participant scores or a claim that the scoring is calibrated for every portfolio.

## Items deliberately left open

Decision-making roles remain as currently written, including decider and the person who made the case against. The Board Ask name and its mid-year framing remain unchanged. Mentor board-chair and board wording also remains unchanged. Financial labels within those screens use the agreed terminology, without redefining the roles or the negotiation exercise.

Yield language has not been changed. The narrative concerns admissions yield and the entering class, while the engine changes a single enrollment index that scales total tuition revenue. Those concepts are not interchangeable. The intended population and timing should be discussed before changing either the narrative or the model.

The proposed order of annual briefing before the four financial cards remains a layout proposal. This terminology revision does not reorder the screen. The more detailed end-of-year results explanation and structured Board Ask rulings also remain outside this implementation.

# Suggested investment card descriptions

Proposals for review. These descriptions have not been applied to the app. Costs, dependencies and actual revenue-start years remain in the separate card labels.

| Card | Current description | Suggested description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Deferred maintenance: roof, HVAC, envelope | Retires $1.2M of a $4.2M backlog. Lowers utilities cost. Deferred maintenance is debt you have already incurred and are choosing not to record. | Repairs roofs, heating and cooling systems, and building exteriors. Addresses $1.2 million of the $4.2 million maintenance backlog and reduces annual utility costs. |
| 2 Business continuity and distributed instruction | Standing capability to deliver instruction and assessment off-site within 72 hours of any disruption. | Funds the systems and arrangements needed to continue teaching and assessment off-site within 72 hours of a disruption. |
| 3 Cybersecurity uplift: MFA, EDR, segmentation, IR retainer | Controls, not policy. Carriers now underwrite on the presence of these specific controls. | Adds stronger sign-in controls, device monitoring, network separation and contracted incident-response support. |
| 4 Cyber liability insurance | Transfers residual risk. Policy contains a controls warranty; a claim without MFA in place is denied. | Purchases cyber insurance for covered incident costs. Claims require the cybersecurity controls in card 3. |
| 5 MS in Clinical AI and Health Informatics, DO dual pathway | Fifty-five seats, stackable with the DO. The largest revenue opportunity in the catalog and the largest recurring obligation. Launching without governance capacity is a reputational exposure, not a shortcut. | Creates a 55-seat master’s pathway that students can combine with the DO program. Requires substantial annual spending before program revenue begins. |
| 6 MS Biomedical Sciences bridge program | Established model, modest margin, feeds the DO applicant pool. Revenue is enrollment-contingent. | Creates a biomedical sciences master’s program that feeds the DO applicant pool. Program revenue is lower when enrollment declines. |
| 7 Faculty development and retention pool | Pure recurring cost with no capital component. Retention is cheaper than replacement and never looks like an accomplishment. | Funds faculty development and retention through an ongoing annual allocation, with no startup cost. |
| 8 OMM department depth and chair succession | Your OMM chair is 63 and no one in the department is credentialed to succeed her. Fellowship line, credentialing support, and a designated successor. Produces nothing visible until the day it is needed. | Funds fellowship training, credentialing and a designated successor for the OMM chair. No current faculty member is qualified to replace the chair. |
| 9 Branch campus development and pro forma | Site, approvals, faculty, and three years of carrying cost before the first class. Will not return inside this planning horizon under any assumption you can defend. | Funds site selection, approvals and faculty preparation for a branch campus. Annual costs begin immediately; program revenue begins after the three-round exercise. |
| 10 Preceptor stipend program | Competing schools are bidding for the same community preceptors. This is a defensive recurring commitment. | Funds ongoing payments to community preceptors to help retain clinical training sites as competing schools increase their payments. |
| 11 Rural and underserved training track | Eligible for the restricted gift. Strong mission alignment and a prerequisite for capturing aligned philanthropy. | Develops a clinical training track serving rural and underserved communities. |
| 12 AI governance framework and faculty upskilling | Policy, oversight structure, assessment integrity standards, and faculty capability. Cheap. Unglamorous. Gates the value of every other AI investment. | Funds AI policies, oversight, assessment-integrity standards and faculty training to support the college’s use of AI. |
| 13 Data infrastructure and institutional research capacity | Warehouse, governance, and two analysts. Grants your team one additional intelligence line in every subsequent signals brief. | Funds a data warehouse, data governance and two analysts. Provides additional institution-specific information in subsequent annual briefs. |
| 14 Advancement officer and campaign feasibility study | Relationship capacity. Major gifts are cultivated over years and do not arrive at institutions with nobody to receive them. | Hires an advancement officer and assesses the feasibility of a fundraising campaign. Builds donor relationships over several years. |
| 15 Scholarship and tuition discount pool | Protects yield when financing conditions tighten. Discounting is a permanent structural decision disguised as a temporary one. | Funds scholarships and tuition discounts to help maintain enrollment when students face tighter borrowing limits. |
